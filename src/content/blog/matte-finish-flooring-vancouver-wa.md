Ten years ago, a new floor was supposed to shine. Glossy hardwood, high-gloss laminate and polished tile were what "new" looked like. Today almost every floor we sell in Vancouver is matte or close to it, across every material: hardwood, engineered wood, luxury vinyl, laminate and porcelain tile.

There are good reasons for that, and a few things to know before you choose. This guide covers what matte actually means, how it works in each type of flooring, and when satin or polished is still the better pick.

If you're choosing a sheen for a site-finished or refinished hardwood floor specifically, our guide to [matte, satin and semi-gloss hardwood finishes](/blog/hardwood-floor-sheen-vancouver-wa/) goes deeper on that decision.

## What "matte" means on a floor

Sheen is how much light a surface reflects. Finish makers measure it in gloss units, and each brand draws its own lines, but roughly:

- **Matte (or "flat," "ultra-matte," "natural")**: very low reflection. The floor looks close to unfinished wood or natural stone.
- **Satin**: a soft, low glow. The most common hardwood finish for years.
- **Semi-gloss**: noticeably shiny.
- **Gloss and polished**: mirror-like reflection.

Two products both labeled "matte" can look different, so always look at a real sample, not a photo.

On LVP, laminate and tile, sheen is built into the surface at the factory. On a site-finished hardwood floor, it comes from the clear top coat, which means it can be changed later.

## Why matte has taken over

**It hides scratches and wear.** This is the big one. Sheen does not change how hard a finish is. What it changes is how visible damage is. A scratch on a glossy floor catches the light from across the room. The same scratch on a matte floor is nearly invisible. With dogs, kids and chairs, that difference shows up within weeks.

**It hides dust, pet hair and footprints.** Shiny floors show every dust bunny and paw print in raking light. Matte floors don't.

**It looks more natural.** Matte wood looks like wood instead of a coating on wood. Matte LVP and laminate look much more like real hardwood than the shiny versions did. Matte porcelain looks like stone.

**It suits Pacific Northwest light.** For much of the year, Vancouver light is low, gray and comes in sideways through big windows. On a glossy floor, that creates glare and long reflections of every window. Matte softens it, so the room feels calmer and the floor color reads true.

**It fits current design.** Warm natural wood tones, white oak and textured surfaces are what most people are choosing right now, and all of them look best with a low sheen. See our guide to [warm wood stain colors](/blog/hardwood-floor-stain-colors-warm-tones-vancouver-wa/).

## Matte in each type of flooring

### Site-finished and refinished hardwood

When we finish a hardwood floor on site, whether it's a new install or a [refinish](/blog/hardwood-floor-refinishing-vancouver-guide/), you choose the sheen of the top coat. Matte water-based polyurethanes are the most popular choice we see. They dry clear, without the amber color of oil-based finishes, so natural and white oak looks stay light.

Some homeowners want an oiled or "raw wood" look, which matte finishes get closest to. Penetrating oil finishes give that look too, but they need more regular maintenance than a polyurethane.

The advantage of a site-finished floor is that sheen isn't permanent. A screen and recoat can take a glossy floor down to matte without sanding to bare wood.

### Prefinished engineered hardwood

Most [engineered hardwood](/blog/engineered-hardwood-flooring-vancouver-wa/) comes from the factory with a UV-cured finish, and most current lines are matte or very low sheen, often with a wire-brushed texture that brings out the grain. Factory finishes are very hard and consistent. Two things to know: the sheen is chosen when you choose the product, and if you recoat it later, the new finish has to be compatible with the factory finish. We check that before any recoat.

### Luxury vinyl plank

LVP has a clear wear layer over a printed image of wood or stone. Older vinyl had a shiny, plastic-looking top. Today most lines have a low-gloss surface with texture embossed into it, and on better lines the texture follows the printed grain (often called embossed-in-register). Run your hand across the plank and you feel the grain where you see it. That's a big part of why current LVP looks so much like hardwood.

Matte LVP is easy to live with. It hides scuffs and doesn't show water spots. Our [luxury vinyl plank installation guide](/blog/luxury-vinyl-plank-installation-vancouver-wa/) covers what it needs underneath.

### Laminate

Laminate has the same story. Current lines from the brands we carry, including Quick-Step, Mohawk and Shaw, are mostly low-gloss with realistic textures. Laminate's hard wear layer already resists scratching better than vinyl, so a matte laminate is one of the most forgiving floors you can put down in a house with large dogs. See our [laminate flooring guide](/blog/laminate-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

### Porcelain and ceramic tile

Tile comes in matte (sometimes called natural or honed-look), satin, and polished or glossy finishes.

- **Matte tile** is the practical choice for floors. It is usually less slippery when wet, hides water spots and dust, and looks like natural stone.
- **Polished tile** looks dramatic and bounces light around, but it is slicker when wet and shows every footprint, water spot and smear. It's better for walls, backsplashes and low-traffic formal spaces.

For any tile floor in a bathroom, entry or laundry room, check the slip rating. Tile with a DCOF (dynamic coefficient of friction) of 0.42 or higher meets the common standard for level indoor floors that may get wet. Our [tile installation guide](/blog/tile-installation-vancouver-wa/) and [bathroom flooring guide](/blog/bathroom-flooring-vancouver-wa/) have more.

## Quick comparison

| Material | How the matte look is made | Can the sheen change later? | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Site-finished hardwood | Matte top coat | Yes, with a recoat or refinish | Oily cleaners leave streaks |
| Prefinished engineered | Factory UV-cured finish | Only with a compatible recoat | Choose sheen when you choose the product |
| LVP | Low-gloss textured wear layer | No | Rubber-backed mats can stain vinyl |
| Laminate | Low-gloss textured wear layer | No | Standing water on non-waterproof lines |
| Porcelain tile | Matte glaze or surface | No | Grout lines show dirt more than the tile |

## Matte vs. satin: which to choose

Satin is the closest alternative, and it's still a great choice. Here's how we usually advise:

- **Choose matte** if you have dogs, young kids, a lot of foot traffic or big windows that rake light across the floor. It's also the right choice if you want the most natural, raw-wood look.
- **Choose satin** if you want a little more glow and depth in the grain, especially on darker or richer stains, or if the floor is in a low-traffic room.
- **Avoid gloss on floors** in most homes. It's beautiful for about a month.

On hardwood, you can also combine them: matte in the busy rooms, or matte everywhere with a satin recoat later if you want more shine.

## Caring for a matte floor

Matte floors are easy to live with, with one catch: the wrong cleaner shows up as smears.

- **Use the cleaner the manufacturer recommends** for your floor type. Most are pH-neutral.
- **Skip oil soaps, waxes and "shine" products.** They leave a film that builds up and makes a matte floor look blotchy and greasy.
- **Dry-mop or vacuum often.** Grit is what scratches every finish. In our wet season, a good mat at each door does more than anything else.
- **Use felt pads** under chairs and furniture.
