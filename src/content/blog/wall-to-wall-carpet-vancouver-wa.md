Most flooring advice now assumes you want hard surface everywhere. Many Vancouver homeowners do, and for the main floor of a busy house that is often the right call. But "should we keep wall-to-wall carpet?" is still one of the questions we hear most in the showroom, usually from someone standing in a bedroom that feels cold and loud since the carpet came out of the rest of the house.

This guide answers that question room by room. It covers when wall-to-wall carpet is still the best floor, when hardwood or LVP with an area rug is better, what you might find under old carpet, how carpet meets hard floors, and what it costs.

If you have already decided on carpet and want to choose one, our guide to [carpet fiber, pile and pad](/blog/carpet-vancouver-wa/) covers that, and [carpet installation in Vancouver](/blog/carpet-installation-vancouver-wa/) walks through the install day.

## Wall-to-wall carpet vs. hard floors with area rugs

The real alternative to wall-to-wall carpet is not a bare hard floor. It is a hard floor with an area rug on it. Here is how the two compare:

| | Wall-to-wall carpet | Hard floor + area rug |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warmth underfoot | Warm edge to edge | Warm on the rug, cold around it |
| Noise between floors | Best available | Fair; depends on rug size and pad |
| Spills and pets | Absorbs; needs prompt cleaning | Hard surface wipes up; rug can be cleaned or replaced |
| Allergies | Holds dust until vacuumed | Easier to keep fully clean |
| Lifespan | 10–15 years for quality carpet | Hardwood lasts decades; LVP 20+ years |
| Installed cost | Lowest, roughly $3–$7/sq ft | Higher, roughly $6–$18/sq ft plus the rug |
| Changing the look | Replace the carpet | Swap the rug |

The pattern is simple. Wall-to-wall carpet wins on comfort, quiet and upfront cost. A hard floor with a rug wins on lifespan, cleanup and flexibility. Which matters more depends on the room.

## Room by room: where wall-to-wall carpet still makes sense

**Bedrooms: carpet's strongest case.** Bare feet on a January morning, a quiet room above the living area, and no foot traffic heavy enough to wear it out quickly. Most households that go hard surface everywhere else keep carpet in the bedrooms, and buyers expect it there.

**Stairs.** Carpeted stairs are quieter and give better grip, which matters with kids, older relatives and dogs. If the stairs are hardwood you love, a runner gives you most of the grip and quiet without hiding the wood.

**Basements and bonus rooms.** Carpet over a good pad takes the chill off a concrete slab and soaks up sound in a media room. Below grade, check for moisture first. A basement that has ever been damp needs a [waterproof floor](/blog/waterproof-flooring-vancouver-wa/) instead.

**Upstairs hallways and landings.** Carpet stops footsteps from carrying into the rooms below. Hallways also take concentrated traffic, so use your most durable carpet here.

**Living rooms: it depends.** In a formal living room or family room used mostly for sitting, wall-to-wall carpet is comfortable and quiet. In an open-concept main floor that runs into the kitchen and entry, a continuous hard floor with a large rug in the seating area usually works better.

**Kitchens, bathrooms, entries and laundry rooms: no.** Carpet absorbs whatever lands on it, and in our wet climate a floor that stays damp becomes a mold problem. These rooms need tile, [luxury vinyl plank](/luxury-vinyl-plank/) or [sheet vinyl](/blog/sheet-vinyl-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

## What is under your old wall-to-wall carpet?

Before replacing carpet in an older Vancouver home, find out what is underneath. Pull up a corner inside a closet: grip the carpet at the edge with pliers, lift it off the tack strip and fold back the pad.

What you might find:

- **Hardwood.** Common in homes built before the 1970s, when oak floors were often carpeted over. If it is there, it may be worth saving. Expect staple holes from the pad and nail holes from the tack strip around the edges. Refinishing usually deals with both, and our guide to [refinishing or replacing hardwood](/blog/refinish-or-replace-hardwood-floors-vancouver-wa/) helps you decide.
- **Plywood or particleboard subfloor.** Common in newer homes. That is a normal base for new carpet, LVP or engineered hardwood. Particleboard does not tolerate water, so it limits what can go over it in wet areas.
- **Concrete.** On slabs and in basements. Fine for carpet, LVP, tile and glue-down or floating floors, after a moisture check.
- **Old sheet vinyl or tile.** In homes built before the 1980s, some older vinyl and its adhesive can contain asbestos. Do not scrape or sand it until it has been tested.

If you find hardwood worth keeping, [hardwood refinishing](/hardwood-refinishing/) is often in the same price range as new carpet over the same area, and the result lasts far longer.

## Where carpet meets a hard floor

Most homes now mix carpet and hard surface, so the places where they meet need planning:

- **Put transitions at doorways.** The change should happen under the door, not halfway across a hallway. That way each room reads as one floor.
- **Match the heights.** Carpet over pad usually sits higher than LVP and lower than some tile installs. The right transition strip depends on the difference, and a large height gap is a trip edge.
- **Doors may need trimming.** Thick carpet and pad can stop a door that cleared the old floor. We check door clearances as part of every install.
- **Do the hard floor first** if both are going in on the same project. The hard surface sets the finished height, and the carpet is fitted to it.

## What wall-to-wall carpet costs in Vancouver

Installed wall-to-wall carpet typically runs roughly **$3 to $7 per square foot**, including the pad and labor. Our [flooring cost guide](/blog/flooring-cost-vancouver-wa/) has ranges for every material.

For a sense of scale, a 12-by-14-foot bedroom is 168 square feet. At that range the carpet, pad and install come to roughly $500 to $1,200. A three-bedroom upstairs with a hallway is often 600 to 900 square feet.

What moves your number:

- **Room layout.** Carpet comes off 12- or 15-foot rolls, so a room's dimensions decide how much is cut off and wasted and where seams go. Several small rooms often need more carpet than one big room of the same total area.
- **Carpet and pad grade.** Nylon costs more than polyester and lasts longer. A dense pad costs a little more and is the cheapest way to make carpet last.
- **Stairs,** priced per step because each one is cut and fitted by hand.
- **Removal of the old carpet and pad,** furniture moving and any subfloor repair.

When you compare quotes, compare the installed total with the pad, removal and stairs listed separately. A low price per square foot with a thin pad is not a bargain.

## Resale: what Clark County buyers expect

If you might sell in the next few years, the rule of thumb is hard surface in the shared spaces and fresh, neutral carpet in the bedrooms. Buyers rarely object to carpet in bedrooms. They often do object to worn carpet in a living room or any carpet in a kitchen or bathroom. Listing agents often recommend replacing tired carpet before photos, because it is one of the cheapest ways to make a house look cared for.

If you are staying for the long term, choose for how you live rather than for a future buyer.
