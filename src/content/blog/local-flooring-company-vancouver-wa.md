Type "flooring companies near me" into Google from anywhere in Clark County and you will get a page full of results that all look roughly the same: a business name, some photos of beautiful floors, a phone number and a button to get a free quote.

They are not the same. Some are flooring companies with a showroom down the road and crews who live here. Some are national chains that hand the installation to a subcontractor. Some are directories that sell your phone number to whoever pays for it. All of them describe themselves as local.

This guide explains how to tell the difference, and why it matters more for flooring than for most purchases. If you have already found a company and want to check them out before you sign, our guide to [hiring a flooring contractor in Vancouver, WA](/blog/how-to-hire-flooring-contractor-vancouver-wa/) covers registration, insurance and contracts. This post is about the step before that: working out who you are actually dealing with.

## Who shows up when you search "flooring near me"

Almost every result falls into one of five groups.

### 1. Local flooring stores that install

A showroom with a street address, staff who sell the floor, and installers who work for or with that company regularly. They measure, sell, install and handle any warranty issues. This is the traditional local flooring company, and what most people picture when they search.

### 2. Big-box home improvement stores

Material is sold in the store. Installation is sold as a program and done by an independent installer who is contracted through the store, often a different crew each job. It can work well for simple jobs. The trade-off is that the person who measures, the person who sells and the person who installs often never talk to each other.

### 3. Shop-at-home and franchise brands

A salesperson comes to your house with a van full of samples. Some are locally owned franchises; others are national companies with a regional office. Installation is usually subcontracted. The convenience is real, but ask who the installer is and where they are based.

### 4. Online retailers

You order boxes of flooring delivered to your driveway. Prices can be sharp, but there is no installation, no measure and no one to call when the color in the box doesn't match the photo on your screen. If you go this route, you are hiring an installer separately and taking on the risk of ordering the wrong quantity.

### 5. Lead sites and referral directories

These are the ones to watch for. They are built to look like a local flooring company, often with your city name in the business name or the web address, but they don't install anything. Your form goes to a marketplace, and one or more contractors who paid for the lead call you back. Sometimes that contractor is good. Often you cannot tell who they are until they show up.

## How to tell if a flooring company is really local

You can usually sort a list of results in ten minutes with these checks.

**A street address you can visit.** Not a P.O. box, not "serving the Vancouver area," and not a suite number in a shared mailbox building. A real flooring company has somewhere you can go and stand on the floors. Look it up on a map and check the street view.

**A showroom.** Flooring is a physical product. Color shifts under different light, texture matters, and samples the size of a playing card don't tell you much. A company that invests in a showroom expects you to come in and compare.

**A phone number answered by the company.** Call it. If you reach someone who can tell you about products and scheduling, good. If you reach a call center that takes your details and promises someone will be in touch, you have found a lead service.

**A straight answer to "who installs?"** Ask whether installation is done by the company's own crews or regular installers, or by whoever is available. Then ask who you call if there is a problem six months from now.

**Years in the area.** A company that has been flooring homes locally for a long time has a reputation to protect. Look at the business's founding date, how far back its reviews go, and whether the reviews mention local neighborhoods and real projects.

**Washington registration.** In Washington, contractors must be registered with the Department of Labor & Industries, and you can look up any contractor's registration, bond and insurance on the L&I website in a couple of minutes. Our [hiring guide](/blog/how-to-hire-flooring-contractor-vancouver-wa/) walks through what to check.

**A realistic service area.** A company that claims to serve every city in two states usually doesn't. A local company can tell you exactly where it works and how long it takes to get there.

## Why "local" matters more for floors than for most things

You can buy a refrigerator from anyone. It arrives, it gets plugged in, and it works the same in Vancouver as in Phoenix. Floors are different, because a floor is only as good as the installation, and the installation depends on the house.

**Our climate is hard on floors.** Clark County and the Portland metro get roughly 40 inches of rain a year, with long, damp winters and dry, furnace-heated air indoors. Wood-based floors expand and contract with those swings. Floors over crawlspaces and concrete slabs pick up moisture from below. A local installer checks for that as a matter of routine. Someone working from a national script may not. Our guide to the [best flooring for the Pacific Northwest climate](/blog/best-flooring-pacific-northwest-climate/) explains what holds up here.

**The housing stock varies street to street.** Vancouver alone runs from mid-century ranches in Hazel Dell and Minnehaha with original oak under the carpet, to 1990s homes in Cascade Park and Fisher's Landing, to new construction in Ridgefield and Battle Ground. Portland has 100-year-old bungalows and four-squares. Each one needs different subfloor prep. Installers who work here every week know what they're likely to find when the old floor comes up.

**Someone has to come back.** Most flooring problems, if they happen, show up in the first year, after the house has gone through a full wet season and a dry one. A company with a showroom fifteen minutes away and a reputation in the community will come back. A subcontractor who was booked through a national program may be on to the next job in another city.

**You can see the product before you commit.** A local showroom lets you compare hardwood, luxury vinyl, laminate, carpet and tile side by side, take samples home, and look at them in your own light before ordering hundreds of square feet. If you're planning a visit, our guide to [visiting a flooring showroom](/blog/flooring-showroom-vancouver-wa/) covers what to bring.

## Questions to ask any flooring company before you book

1. Where is your showroom, and can I come in?
2. Do you install the floors you sell? Who exactly will be in my house?
3. Who measures, and is the measurement included in the quote?
4. Is the quote itemized, including tear-out, subfloor prep, transitions, trim and stairs?
5. Do you check the subfloor for moisture and flatness before installing?
6. What warranty do you give on the installation, separate from the manufacturer's product warranty?
7. If something goes wrong after the job, who do I call, and how fast do you respond?
8. How long have you been doing flooring here, and can I see reviews from nearby jobs?

A real local flooring company will answer every one of these without hesitating. If the answers are vague, keep looking.

## What a fair quote from a local company includes

Whoever you hire, the quote should list the material, labor, removal and disposal of the old floor, subfloor prep, underlayment or moisture barrier, transitions, baseboard or quarter-round work, and any stairs. Comparing a local company's itemized quote with a big-box per-square-foot price is comparing two different things. Get everything in writing and compare total to total.
