If you've searched for a vinyl or laminate flooring installer near Gresham, you've probably noticed that almost everyone sells the same handful of brands. What's different is the installation. Two crews can put the same LVP in the same house and get completely different floors: one flat, quiet and tight at every seam, the other hollow in spots, with gaps opening at the doorways by spring.

This guide is for Gresham homeowners choosing both the floor and the installer. It covers what Gresham homes typically need, the difference between LVP, laminate and sheet vinyl, what good prep looks like, and what a fair quote should include.

## What Gresham homes are like underfoot

Gresham's housing is newer than Portland's. The city's median construction year is around 1981, and much of it went up across Powell Valley, Pleasant Valley, Rockwood and Kelly Creek between the 1970s and 1990s. That shapes what we find when we pull up the old floor:

- **Split-level and tri-level layouts.** Many Gresham homes from this era have several short runs of stairs between levels. Each stair needs its own tread and nosing, and a good installer plans the transitions so every level lines up.
- **Particleboard underlayment** under the original sheet vinyl and carpet, especially in kitchens and baths. It's fine under carpet. Under a floating floor it can be okay if it's flat and dry, but it swells permanently where it has ever gotten wet, and those spots have to be cut out and replaced.
- **Several layers.** Carpet over pad, sheet vinyl over another sheet vinyl, or vinyl tile under it all. Each layer adds height and affects door clearances and transitions.
- **Concrete in newer homes and additions.** Garages converted to living space and slab-on-grade sections need moisture testing and a vapor barrier under anything floating.
- **A long, wet season.** Gresham averages close to 48 inches of rain a year, more than Portland. Wet boots, wet dogs and damp crawlspaces all argue for water-resistant floors in entries and lower levels.

## LVP, laminate or sheet vinyl?

### Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) and luxury vinyl tile (LVT)

Rigid-core LVP is the most popular floor we install in Gresham. The core is waterproof, it's quiet and comfortable underfoot, and it floats over most existing floors. Luxury vinyl tile is the same material in a stone or tile look, which is great for bathrooms and laundry rooms. See our [luxury vinyl tile guide](/blog/luxury-vinyl-tile-vancouver-wa/).

**Best for:** kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms, entries, basements, converted garages, rentals and whole-house installs.

### Laminate

Laminate has a wood-fiber core with a very hard wear surface on top. It resists scratches better than vinyl, feels firmer and more like wood, and usually costs a little less. Many current lines are water-resistant, and some are rated waterproof against surface spills, though the core is still wood-based. Our [waterproof laminate vs. LVP guide](/blog/waterproof-laminate-vs-lvp-vancouver-wa/) explains the difference.

**Best for:** living rooms, bedrooms, hallways and family rooms, especially with large dogs.

### Sheet vinyl

Sheet vinyl comes in wide rolls, so a small kitchen, bath or laundry room can be covered with few or no seams. That makes it very water-tight and very affordable. Many people call it "linoleum," though true linoleum is a different product made from linseed oil. See our [sheet vinyl guide](/blog/sheet-vinyl-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

**Best for:** small bathrooms, laundry rooms and rentals on a tight budget.

## What a good installer does that a fast one doesn't

Most failures with vinyl and laminate floors aren't product failures. They're installation failures. Here's what to expect from a careful crew, and what to ask any installer you're considering.

**1. Checks flatness, not just cleanliness.** Floating floors need a flat subfloor. Most manufacturers ask for no more than about 3/16 of an inch of variation over 10 feet. Over a dip, a floating floor flexes when you walk on it, and over time the locking joints wear and open up. A good installer runs a straightedge across every room, then grinds high spots and fills low ones before the first plank goes down.

**2. Tests moisture over concrete.** Slabs give off moisture. Over concrete, the installer should check moisture and put down the vapor barrier the manufacturer requires.

**3. Deals with soft or swollen underlayment.** In kitchens, baths and around toilets, old particleboard is often swollen from past leaks. It should be cut out and replaced, not covered.

**4. Leaves the expansion gap.** Floating floors expand and contract. They need a gap at every wall, hidden under baseboard or quarter-round. They also cannot be pinned down under kitchen islands or heavy built-ins.

**5. Plans the layout.** Rows should run in the direction that looks best from the main sightlines, and the first and last rows shouldn't end in thin slivers. In split-level homes, the floor direction should be continuous from level to level.

**6. Finishes stairs properly.** On split-level stairs, each step gets a fitted tread and a matching stair nose that is glued and fastened. Stairs are where shortcuts show first. See our guide to [hardwood stairs](/blog/hardwood-stairs-vancouver-wa/) for how stair work is approached.

**7. Undercuts door casings and handles transitions.** Door casings should be undercut so the planks slide underneath, rather than being caulked around. Transitions to carpet, tile or a neighboring floor should be planned for height.

## Old vinyl and asbestos

Vinyl tile, sheet vinyl backing and the black mastic used to glue them down often contained asbestos into the 1980s. Intact and left alone, they aren't a hazard. Scraping, sanding or breaking them is.

For most residential renovation in homes built before January 1, 2004, Oregon DEQ requires an asbestos survey by an accredited inspector before materials such as flooring are removed. Owner-occupants doing their own work in their own home are the main exception. Check the current DEQ rules for your situation.

In many Gresham homes the simplest answer is not to remove the old vinyl at all. If it's flat, sound and well bonded, floating LVP or laminate can go right over it.

## What a good quote should include

Low per-square-foot prices often leave out the work that makes a floor last. When you compare quotes, check that each one includes:

- **Measured square footage** plus a stated waste allowance
- **Removal and disposal** of the old flooring
- **Subfloor prep**: leveling, underlayment replacement and moisture barrier as needed
- **Stairs**, priced per step
- **Moving furniture** and appliances, or a note that you'll handle it
- **Baseboard or quarter-round, transitions and door undercutting**
- **A written workmanship warranty**, separate from the manufacturer's product warranty

Our guide to [hiring a flooring contractor](/blog/how-to-hire-flooring-contractor-vancouver-wa/) has more questions to ask, and [what to expect on installation day](/blog/what-to-expect-flooring-installation/) walks through the day itself.

## How long it takes

- **One room:** usually one day.
- **A typical main level:** two to four days.
- **Split-level or tri-level homes:** longer, because of the stairs and transitions between levels.
- **Add time** for old flooring removal, particleboard replacement and leveling.

Floating LVP and laminate can be walked on as soon as they're installed. Furniture can usually go back the same day.
