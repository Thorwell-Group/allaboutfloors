A meaningful part of our work in Clark County is not for homeowners. It is for the builders, general contractors and remodelers who put floors into the homes they are building, and who need a flooring partner that shows up on schedule, installs to spec, and does not generate callbacks.

If you build or remodel in Vancouver, Ridgefield, Battle Ground, Camas or across the Portland metro, this is how we work with you.

## What builders actually need from a flooring subcontractor

It is not a big selection. Homeowners want selection. Builders want four things:

1. **Schedule reliability.** Flooring sits near the end of the critical path, and a crew that shows up late holds up trim, paint touch-up, and the closing.
2. **Consistent product.** The same floor, in the same color, in every unit of a phase, available for the whole phase.
3. **Clean installation with a short punch list.** No hollow spots, no lippage, transitions that are level, and a jobsite left clean.
4. **Someone to handle the buyer's upgrade conversation** so the superintendent does not have to.

Everything below is in service of those four.

## Allowances and upgrades

Most production and semi-custom builds specify a **flooring allowance**: a base-grade product in each area, priced into the home. The buyer can accept the base or pay the difference to upgrade.

Two things make allowances work well:

**The base grade has to be a floor you are willing to warrant.** A builder-grade LVP with a 6 mil wear layer will look tired inside the warranty period and generate calls. Setting the base at a 12 mil LVP or a properly padded carpet costs a little more per foot and removes most of the callbacks. Our [vinyl buyer's guide](/blog/vinyl-flooring-vancouver-wa/) explains what the wear-layer spec means.

**The upgrade path has to be easy for the buyer.** We handle this directly: the buyer meets us, sees the base product and the upgrade options together, and gets a clear upgrade price. You get the selection sheet. Your super never has to explain the difference between engineered and solid hardwood.

For custom homes, the same logic applies without the allowance structure: we meet the client, help them select, and give you a firm installed price per area.

## Sequencing and site conditions

Flooring goes wrong in new construction for one reason far more than any other: it went in before the building was ready.

**The house has to be dry.** Wood, laminate and even rigid-core vinyl react to moisture. In a new build, the framing lumber, the drywall mud, the paint and especially the concrete are all still releasing water. The interior needs to be closed in, with HVAC running and holding normal living temperature and humidity, before hard-surface flooring goes down.

**New slabs need testing.** Concrete releases moisture for months after the pour. Every hard-surface floor has a moisture limit from its manufacturer, and exceeding it voids the warranty and eventually lifts the floor. We test slabs before installation. If a slab is not ready, we tell you and we reschedule, because installing over a wet slab means doing the job twice.

**Hardwood needs acclimation.** Solid and engineered wood is delivered to the site and sits in the conditioned space for several days before installation so it reaches equilibrium with the house. That only works if the house is actually conditioned. Wood acclimated in a cold, damp shell moves after the heat comes on. Our guide to [engineered vs. solid hardwood](/blog/engineered-vs-solid-hardwood-vancouver/) covers why engineered is usually the right call in the Pacific Northwest.

**Order of operations.** Hard surfaces after drywall, prime and first coat of paint; before final trim and final paint. Carpet last, after painters are out. Tile in wet areas can go earlier once the substrate is ready, but grout needs cure time before other trades walk on it.

## Products that work in Clark County new construction

**Luxury vinyl plank** is the default main-level floor in new construction here, and for good reason: waterproof, fast to install, tolerant of the moisture swings a new home goes through in its first year, and available in consistent large quantities. Rigid-core product handles slab installations well. See our [LVP page](/luxury-vinyl-plank/).

**Engineered hardwood** for the main level where the buyer wants real wood. Stable over slabs and radiant heat, and available prefinished for consistency across units. Solid hardwood belongs in custom work over a wood subfloor with a fully conditioned house.

**Carpet** in bedrooms and bonus rooms. The pad is where builder-grade carpet fails first; a proper pad spec removes most early complaints. See our [carpet page](/carpet-flooring/).

**Porcelain tile** in bathrooms, laundry and mudrooms. Large-format tile needs a flat substrate, so the subfloor spec matters.

For multi-family and light commercial, our [commercial flooring](/commercial-flooring/) team handles phased installs and commercial-grade product.

## Remodelers: working around occupied homes

Remodel work has its own demands, and we do a lot of it with general contractors across Vancouver and Portland.

- **Sequencing with cabinets and countertops.** Flooring under cabinets or up to them is a real decision with cost implications. We coordinate with your cabinet and [countertop](/countertops/) schedule so nobody is waiting.
- **Tear-out and disposal.** We remove and haul the old floor. If your crew has already done demo, we price accordingly.
- **Matching existing floors.** Extending hardwood into a new addition, or matching an existing LVP, is often possible and we will tell you when it is not.
- **Occupied-home logistics.** We phase installs room by room so the client stays in the house.
