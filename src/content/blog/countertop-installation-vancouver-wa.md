Countertops are one of the few upgrades that change how a kitchen looks and how it works at the same time, and the material you pick determines both. Here is the honest comparison we give Vancouver homeowners in the showroom.

## Quartz: the low-maintenance default

Engineered quartz is crushed natural quartz bound with resin, which makes it non-porous. That single property is why it has taken over most kitchens.

Non-porous means it never needs sealing, and it does not absorb the things that stain natural stone — red wine, oil, citrus, coffee. For a household that cooks properly and does not want to think about the counter afterwards, this is the practical answer.

Because it is manufactured, the pattern is consistent, so what you see on the sample is what arrives. That is a genuine advantage if you want a specific look, and a limitation if you want the one-off character of real stone.

The one caution worth knowing: quartz does not like sustained heat. A pan straight off the burner can mark it. Trivets, not bare pans.

## Granite and natural stone: real material, real upkeep

Granite is quarried, so every slab is unique. You are not choosing from a catalogue photograph — you are choosing a specific slab. For a lot of people that is exactly the point, and no engineered product replicates it.

The commitment is sealing. Granite is porous to a degree that varies by stone, and an unsealed or under-sealed counter will take on stains. How often depends on the specific granite, which is why we would rather advise you on the slab you choose than quote a blanket interval.

Marble deserves a specific warning. It is beautiful, it is softer than granite, and it etches on contact with anything acidic — lemon juice, vinegar, wine. Etching is not a stain; it is a dulled spot in the surface. In a working kitchen it will happen. Plenty of people accept that as patina, and plenty of people are extremely unhappy about it six months in. Know which one you are before you commit.

## Solid surface: seamless and repairable

Solid surface materials can be joined almost invisibly and shaped into an integrated sink, which makes cleaning genuinely easier because there is no rim to catch anything.

It is softer than stone, so it scratches. The compensation is that those scratches buff out — it is one of the few counter materials that can be renewed in place rather than replaced. For a busy family kitchen where things get put down hard, that repairability is worth real money.

## Laminate: better than its reputation

Laminate has come a long way from the countertops people remember from the eighties. Modern products with detailed edge profiles look considerably better than they used to, and the price difference against stone is substantial.

The limits are the same as they always were: it cannot take heat, and damage cannot be repaired, only replaced. For a rental property, a laundry room, or a kitchen you plan to redo properly in a few years, it is a sensible choice rather than a compromise.

## What template day actually involves

This is the part people are least prepared for.

Your countertops are not cut to a plan drawing. Someone comes to your home and templates the actual space, because kitchen walls are never quite square and the fit has to account for reality. That template goes to fabrication, the slabs are cut to it, and then installation happens.

Which means there is a genuine gap between template and install, usually a couple of weeks. Your old counters have to be out for templating in most cases, so plan for a kitchen that is partly out of action across that period rather than for a one-day job.

## Sequencing with flooring

If you are doing floors and counters together — and it is a good time to do both — the order matters.

The main question is whether the new flooring runs under the cabinets or stops at them. That decision affects the finished height of the counter, the clearance for a dishwasher or range, and how the toe-kick finishes. Getting it wrong is the kind of mistake that only shows up when an appliance will not slide into its slot.
