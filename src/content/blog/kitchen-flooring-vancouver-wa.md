A kitchen floor takes more abuse in a year than a bedroom floor takes in a decade. It gets stood on for hours in the same three square feet, splashed daily, hit with dropped cast iron, and, sooner or later, flooded by a dishwasher or fridge line that let go overnight.

So the question isn't really "what looks good in a kitchen." It's "what survives a kitchen." Here's how we think about it for Vancouver and Clark County homes.

## The three ways kitchen floors actually fail

Almost every kitchen floor we replace failed one of these three ways:

- **Slow water at the appliances.** A fridge line or dishwasher weep that drips for months before anyone notices. This is the big one, and it's what kills laminate and solid hardwood.
- **The traffic lane.** A visible wear path from the sink to the fridge to the range. Gloss finishes show it first; matte hides it longest.
- **Impact.** A dropped pan chips tile or dents softer wood species. Nothing is immune, but some floors take it better than others.

Choose for those three, and the aesthetics take care of themselves.

## Waterproof luxury vinyl plank: the default for most kitchens

For the majority of Vancouver kitchens, [luxury vinyl plank](/luxury-vinyl-plank/) is the right answer. The core is plastic-based, so a dishwasher leak that sits overnight doesn't swell the planks the way it would ruin a wood-core floor. It's softer underfoot than tile, which matters when you're prepping dinner for an hour. And a rigid-core (SPC) plank handles a dropped pot without chipping.

The honest limitation: "waterproof" describes the plank, not the installation. Standing water can still find its way to the subfloor at the perimeter of a floating floor. Wipe up spills; don't let a leak run for a week.

## Porcelain tile: the most durable, with two tradeoffs

[Tile](/tile-flooring/) is genuinely waterproof, immune to heat, and effectively permanent. If you're putting in radiant heat, tile is the obvious pairing.

The two tradeoffs are real, though. Tile is hard on your feet and your back over long cooking sessions, and it's hard on anything you drop, glassware doesn't survive a fall onto porcelain. Grout also needs sealing and eventually shows in a busy kitchen. Large-format tile with tight joints minimizes the grout you have to live with.

## Engineered hardwood: yes, if the kitchen is open to the living room

Open-plan homes create a design problem: a transition strip in the middle of a great room looks like a mistake. Running the same [hardwood](/hardwood-flooring/) through the kitchen solves it.

Engineered is the version to use. Its plywood core moves far less with humidity than solid wood, which matters here, where indoor air swings from damp in January to dry in August. Pick a matte or satin sheen, glossy hardwood in a kitchen shows every footprint and every wear lane. Our guide to [choosing a hardwood sheen](/blog/hardwood-floor-sheen-vancouver-wa/) walks through that decision in detail.

What engineered hardwood will not tolerate is a slow leak. If your appliances are older, weigh that honestly.

## Laminate: only if you're realistic about it

Modern water-resistant [laminate](/laminate-flooring/) looks excellent and costs less than LVP. In a low-risk kitchen with newer appliances and a household that wipes spills, it's a defensible budget choice.

But the core is wood fiber. Standing water at a seam will swell it, and a swollen laminate plank cannot be repaired, it has to be replaced. If the kitchen is the reason you're re-flooring in the first place, spend the extra dollar or two per square foot on LVP. See our [LVP vs. laminate comparison](/blog/lvp-vs-laminate-rainy-climate/) for the full breakdown.

## What we do differently around cabinets and appliances

Two details matter more than the material you pick:

- **Floating floors stop at the toe kick.** LVP and laminate need room to expand. If planks are trapped under a cabinet run or a heavy island, the floor has nowhere to go and it buckles. We plan the layout around this before the first plank goes down.
- **Appliances come out.** Flooring only up to the front edge of the dishwasher creates a lip that makes future appliance replacement a demolition job. We pull them, floor underneath, and reset.
