Search for carpet stores in Vancouver, WA and you get a mix of national chains, big-box home centers, shop-at-home services and local flooring showrooms, all promising the best selection and the lowest price. Most of them sell carpet from the same handful of mills. What differs is everything around the carpet: the pad they put under it, who installs it, how the job is measured, and who answers the phone if a seam shows six months later.

This guide is about how to compare carpet stores, from a company that is one. We will be upfront about where we fit and where another kind of store might suit you better. For help choosing the carpet itself, see our guide to [carpet fiber, pile and pad](/blog/carpet-vancouver-wa/).

## Three kinds of places sell carpet

**Big-box home centers.** The selection is narrower and leans toward entry-level and mid-range polyester. The advertised price is usually low, and installation is subcontracted to whichever local crew the program assigns. This can work for a single rental bedroom or a simple, price-driven job. The risk is accountability: the store sold it, a third party measured it, another installed it, and a problem can bounce between them.

**National shop-at-home brands.** A salesperson brings samples to your home, measures and quotes on the spot. It is convenient, and seeing samples in your own light is the right way to choose carpet. The quote is often built around a large discount from a high starting price, with pressure to sign the same day. Treat any price that expires when the salesperson leaves as a reason to slow down.

**Local flooring showrooms.** A specialty store typically carries the full range from several mills, including the better nylon and wool lines the other two channels rarely stock, and employs people who work with carpet every day. The good ones also bring samples to your home and install with crews they know. Quality varies, which is the reason for the rest of this guide.

## Compare the installed price, never the price per square foot

Carpet advertising is built on the square-foot price of the carpet alone, and that number tells you almost nothing. Every carpet quote you collect should itemize:

- The carpet: manufacturer, style name, fiber and color, so you can compare like with like
- The pad: thickness and density, not just "premium pad"
- Installation labor
- Stairs, priced per step
- Removal and disposal of the old carpet and pad
- Furniture moving
- Transitions, tack strip and any subfloor repair

"Free installation" deserves the same scrutiny. Labor is never free. It is built into the material price, and the offer often covers only basic installation, with stairs, furniture and disposal added afterward. Our guide to [how flooring sales work](/blog/flooring-deals-vancouver-wa-how-sales-work/) breaks down the math. A free-install offer can still be a fair total. You only find out by comparing totals.

## Ask about the pad first

The pad is the easiest place for a store to make a low quote look good. It is hidden the day the carpet goes down, and you will never see it again.

Pad absorbs the impact of every footstep. Put a good carpet on a thin, low-density pad and the fibers take that impact instead, crush early, and the carpet looks worn years before it should. Most carpet manufacturers also set minimum pad requirements, and a pad below them can void the wear warranty.

Ask every store the same question: what pad is included in this price, and what are its thickness and density? If the answer is vague, the pad is where the quote was trimmed. Our [carpet buying guide](/blog/carpet-vancouver-wa/) explains what to look for, including why stairs need a denser pad rather than a thicker one.

## Ask who installs it

Carpet is only as good as its installation. Carpet that is not power-stretched properly develops ripples within a couple of years. Seams placed in the wrong spot show forever. Both are installation problems, not product problems, and neither is covered by the carpet manufacturer.

Questions worth asking:

- Are the installers your own crews or subcontractors assigned per job?
- Is the carpet power-stretched, not just knee-kicked into place?
- Who do I call if a seam opens or a ripple appears, and what does your installation warranty cover?
- Is your company registered, bonded and insured with Washington L&I?

## Ask how it will be measured and where the seams will go

Carpet comes off a roll, most often 12 feet wide. Any room wider than the roll needs a seam, and the way a job is laid out determines both how much carpet you pay for and where those seams end up.

A careful store measures in person and can show you a seam plan before you commit. Good seam placement keeps seams out of doorways and main walking paths, runs them away from windows where low light would catch them, and keeps the pile running the same direction through connected rooms. An estimate worked out from your own rough dimensions is fine for budgeting. It is not fine for ordering.

Patterned carpet needs extra material so the pattern lines up at the seams. If one quote is noticeably lower on a patterned style, check whether pattern match was included.

## Take samples home

Carpet changes color more than any other floor between a showroom and a living room. Showroom lighting is bright and even. Your home has north light, warm lamps and, for much of a Vancouver winter, gray daylight at four in the afternoon. Pile direction also shifts the color: the same carpet reads lighter from one side of the room and darker from the other.

Take the final two or three candidates home. Look at them on the floor, not held up in your hand, in the morning and in the evening, next to your wall color and your largest piece of furniture. A store that will not lend samples or bring them to you is asking you to guess.

## Read the warranty for what it requires

Carpet warranties sound generous: stain, wear and texture retention coverage measured in decades. They also come with conditions, and a good store explains them before you buy rather than after you file a claim. Common ones include professional installation over a qualifying pad, periodic professional hot-water-extraction cleaning with receipts kept, and exclusions for stairs and for certain kinds of stains. None of that makes the warranty worthless. It means the warranty is worth exactly as much as your ability to meet its terms, so ask what they are.

## A checklist for comparing carpet stores

- Does the quote itemize carpet, pad, labor, stairs, tear-out, furniture and transitions?
- Can they tell you the pad's thickness and density?
- Do they measure in person and show you a seam plan?
- Will they get samples into your home?
- Do they install with crews they know, and is there a written installation warranty?
- Are they registered with Washington L&I, and do their [reviews](/reviews/) describe how problems were handled?
- Is there pressure to sign today? There should not be.
