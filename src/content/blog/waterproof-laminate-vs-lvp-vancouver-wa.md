Five years ago this comparison was easy. LVP was waterproof and laminate wasn't, so anywhere water might show up, you chose vinyl. Then the major laminate brands came out with waterproof lines, and now the two floors look almost the same on a showroom shelf and both say "waterproof" on the box.

They aren't the same floor, and in a Vancouver house, where wet boots, wet dogs and wet raincoats come through the door for most of the year, the differences matter. This guide explains what "waterproof" covers for each, where each one wins, and which we recommend room by room.

If you're comparing standard laminate with LVP, start with our original guide to [LVP vs. laminate in a rainy climate](/blog/lvp-vs-laminate-rainy-climate/). This post goes deeper on the newer waterproof laminate.

## What's actually different: the core

Both floors are built in layers: a printed design layer with a clear wear layer on top, a core, and often an attached pad. The core is where they split.

- **LVP** has a core of flexible vinyl, wood-plastic composite (WPC) or stone-plastic composite (SPC). None of these absorb water. You could leave a plank in a bucket and it would come out the same.
- **Waterproof laminate** has a high-density wood-fiber (HDF) core, the same basic material as standard laminate, but treated to repel water and paired with tighter, sealed locking joints and coated edges. Water stays on top instead of wicking into the seams.

That difference is why the warranties read differently. LVP is waterproof, period. Waterproof laminate is waterproof on the surface for a limited time.

## What "waterproof" covers, and what it doesn't

**Waterproof laminate warranties** generally cover topical water: spills, splashes, pet accidents, wet shoes. Most give you a window to clean it up, often somewhere between 24 and 72 hours depending on the brand and line. They typically exclude flooding, standing water and water coming from below, such as a leaking dishwasher line or a damp slab. Some lines require the perimeter or the expansion gap to be sealed in wet rooms.

**LVP** isn't harmed by water on top no matter how long it sits.

**Neither one protects the subfloor.** This is the part most people miss. If a toilet supply line or dishwasher leaks under the floor, water spreads across the subfloor underneath the planks. A waterproof floor can actually hide a leak longer, because nothing swells on the surface. Either floor has to come up so the subfloor can dry. The good news is that both are floating click-lock floors, so in many cases the planks can be taken up, dried and reinstalled.

The practical rule: waterproof laminate is a big step up from old laminate and handles everyday water in a kitchen or entry with no trouble. LVP is the one to choose when water might sit for a while or come from below.

## Where waterproof laminate wins

**Scratch resistance.** Laminate's top layer is a hard wear surface rated on an abrasion scale, usually AC3 for homes and AC4 or AC5 for heavy use. It shrugs off dog claws, sliding chairs and the fine grit we track in all winter better than most vinyl wear layers. For a household with large dogs, that's a real advantage.

**Feel and look.** Laminate is stiffer and feels more like a wood floor underfoot. The best lines have deep embossed textures and long, wide planks that are very hard to tell from hardwood. Our [wide plank flooring guide](/blog/wide-plank-flooring-vancouver-wa/) covers the wide-plank options in both.

**Sunrooms and big south-facing windows.** Laminate stays stable in direct sun and heat. Some LVP, especially darker colors, can expand in strong sun behind large glass and needs a bigger expansion gap or a product rated for it.

**Price.** Waterproof laminate usually costs a little less than comparable LVP.

## Where LVP wins

**Water, no fine print.** Kitchens with a dishwasher and ice maker, laundry rooms, full bathrooms, mudrooms and basements are where we still lean toward LVP. Nothing about the plank can be ruined by water.

**Basements and slabs.** Concrete gives off moisture, and Clark County basements tend to be damp. LVP handles a slab with a proper moisture barrier better than any wood-based product.

**Comfort and sound.** LVP is quieter to walk on and a little softer, which matters in upstairs bedrooms and condos.

**Accidents.** Puppies, older pets and kids in potty training. Urine that gets into a seam and sits overnight is the toughest test there is, and LVP handles it best. Our [pet-friendly flooring guide](/blog/pet-friendly-scratch-resistant-flooring-vancouver-wa/) goes deeper.

**Dents.** Rigid SPC cores resist dents from dropped pans and heavy furniture well.

## Installation is similar, with a few differences

Both are floating click-lock floors installed over a flat subfloor, so the process is much the same. Our [laminate installation guide](/blog/laminate-flooring-installation-vancouver-wa/) and [LVP installation guide](/blog/luxury-vinyl-plank-installation-vancouver-wa/) walk through each. The differences that matter:

- **Flatness.** Laminate's stiffer core is a little less forgiving of dips and humps in the subfloor. Expect leveling in older homes either way.
- **Underlayment.** Laminate needs a good underlayment to avoid a hollow, clicky sound, and a moisture barrier over concrete. Many LVP lines have a pad attached.
- **Wet-room details.** Some waterproof laminate lines require sealing the perimeter with silicone in kitchens and baths to keep the warranty. We follow the manufacturer's instructions for the exact product.
- **Cutting.** Laminate is cut with a saw and makes dust. Most rigid LVP can be scored and snapped, which keeps the house cleaner on install day.

## The short answer

- **Choose waterproof laminate** for living areas and bedrooms, especially with big dogs, sun-filled rooms, or if you want the floor that feels most like wood for the money.
- **Choose LVP** for full baths, laundry rooms, basements, slabs, rentals and any room where water could sit or come from underneath.
- **Use both** when the house has both kinds of rooms. That's what we do for a lot of Vancouver families.
