A flooring showroom can be a lot. Walls of carpet, racks of hardwood and vinyl boards, tile on every surface, and somewhere in there is the floor you'll live on for the next fifteen years. People walk in excited and walk out an hour later more confused than when they arrived.

It doesn't have to go that way. With the right things in your phone and a plan for how to spend the hour, a showroom visit is the fastest way to go from "we need new floors" to a shortlist of two or three real options. This guide covers how to prepare for a flooring showroom visit in Vancouver, WA, and what to ask once you're there.

If you're still deciding *which* store to visit, start with our guide to [choosing a flooring store in Vancouver, WA](/blog/how-to-choose-flooring-store-vancouver-wa/). This post is about what to do once you've picked one.

## Why go to a showroom at all

You can browse thousands of floors online. What you can't do online is:

- **See the color in real light.** Screens are calibrated differently, and product photos are edited. Oak that looks honey-gold online can look orange in person, and "greige" vinyl can lean pink or green.
- **Feel the texture.** Embossed vinyl, wire-brushed hardwood and textured porcelain all look flat in a photo. In person you can tell straight away whether you like the feel underfoot.
- **See a full-size board or tile.** A 2-inch swatch hides how much variation there is from board to board. A full plank, or a display of several, shows you what the whole floor will look like.
- **Compare materials side by side.** Hardwood next to LVP next to laminate next to tile, in the same light, answers a lot of questions quickly.
- **Get answers from someone who installs.** A good showroom is staffed by people who know what happens to each product after it leaves the store.

## When to go

Visit before you have a deadline. Two to six weeks ahead of when you want the floor in gives you time to take samples home, get an accurate measurement, order material and schedule installation without rushing. Some products are stocked locally. Others take a week or more to arrive.

The best time is after you have a rough idea of scope (which rooms, roughly how big) but before you've fallen for a specific product online. Our guide to [planning a flooring remodel in Clark County](/blog/flooring-remodel-clark-county-wa/) covers the order of work if floors are part of a bigger project.

## What to bring to a flooring showroom

You don't need much, but these few things make the visit far more useful.

**Photos of each room, taken in daylight.** Get the whole floor, the walls and the cabinets or furniture that will stay. Include the doorways where the new floor will meet another floor.

**Rough room sizes.** Length and width of each room is fine. You don't need an exact measurement. That comes later, and we do it for you. Rough sizes let the consultant give you a ballpark for each option.

**Something you have to match.** A cabinet door, a drawer front, a paint chip, a spare piece of trim, a tile from the bathroom next door. Holding a sample against the real thing settles more decisions than anything else.

**What the floor sits on.** Is the room over a crawlspace, over a concrete slab (a basement or a ground-floor addition), or upstairs? Is there radiant heat? In the Pacific Northwest this decides more than people expect. Some floors don't belong on a damp slab, and some need a specific moisture barrier.

**Who uses the room.** Dogs, kids, a rolling office chair, a home daycare, an entry that sees wet boots six months of the year. This is how a consultant narrows a wall of samples down to the right handful.

**A budget range.** Not to be upsold, but so you can be shown options that fit. A range of installed prices per room is more useful than a per-square-foot number. For typical ranges, see our [Vancouver flooring cost guide](/blog/flooring-cost-vancouver-wa/).

## How to use your hour

**Start with the rooms, not the products.** Spend the first ten minutes describing the house and how you live in it. A good consultant will ask about moisture, pets and traffic before pulling a single sample.

**Narrow by material first, then color.** Decide whether this is a hardwood, LVP, laminate, carpet or tile job before you start choosing colors. Comparing a walnut hardwood to a gray vinyl to a cream carpet at the same time is how people end up overwhelmed.

**Get to three, not ten.** Pick three finalists per room. More than that and it gets harder to decide, not easier.

**Look at samples near a window.** Ask to take boards to the door or the brightest part of the showroom. Then look again under the overhead lights. If a color only looks good in one of them, notice that.

**Stand on it.** Take your shoes off and walk on the hardwood, vinyl and carpet. Feel the difference between a floating floor and a glued one, and between a thick pad and a thin one.

**Look at the edges.** Bevel or no bevel, how the boards meet, and how a carpet seam would look. Small details like these change how the whole floor reads.

## What to ask about each material

| Material | Ask about |
| --- | --- |
| [Hardwood](/hardwood-flooring/) | Solid or engineered? How thick is the wear layer, and how many times can it be refinished? Will it suit my subfloor? |
| [Luxury vinyl plank](/luxury-vinyl-plank/) | How thick is the wear layer (in mils)? Rigid core or flexible? Floating or glue-down? Is the attached pad enough? |
| [Laminate](/laminate-flooring/) | Is it rated waterproof or water-resistant, and what exactly does the warranty cover? What's the AC (abrasion) rating? |
| [Carpet](/carpet-flooring/) | What fiber is it? What pad do you recommend for this carpet and this room? Is it stain or pet protected? |
| [Tile](/tile-flooring/) | Porcelain or ceramic? Is it rated for floors? What's the slip rating for a wet room? What grout color would you use? |

Two questions apply to everything: *What does this product need underneath it?* and *How do you install it in a house like mine?* The answers tell you whether the person helping you understands installation or just sells boxes.

## Take samples home

This is the step people skip, and it's the most important one. Take your finalists home and put them on the floor in the room where they'll go. Look at them in the morning, the afternoon and at night. Put them next to the cabinets and the trim. Vancouver's light from October to April is flat and gray, and a floor that looked warm in the showroom can look cold at home, or the other way round. Living with a sample for a few days tells you more than an hour in any store.

## Showroom visit or in-home estimate?

| | Showroom visit | In-home estimate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best when | You're undecided on material or want to see everything | You've narrowed it down and want a firm price |
| What you see | The full range, side by side | Samples in your own rooms and light |
| Measurement | Rough, from your numbers | Exact, measured by us |
| Subfloor and moisture check | No | Yes |
| Leave with | A shortlist and samples | A written, itemized quote |

Most of our customers do both. They come into the showroom to narrow the choices, then we come to the house to measure, check the subfloor, look at the finalists in place and write the quote.

## What happens after the showroom

1. **You take samples home** and live with them for a few days.
2. **We measure the rooms**, check the subfloor for flatness and moisture, and look at transitions, stairs and doorways.
3. **You get a written, itemized quote** with material, labor, tear-out, prep and trim listed separately. If financing helps, see our [financing options](/financing/).
4. **Material is ordered** and acclimated if the product needs it.
5. **Installation day.** Our guide to [what to expect during a flooring installation](/blog/what-to-expect-flooring-installation/) covers how to get the house ready.
