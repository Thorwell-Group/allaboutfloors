Most people searching for flooring installation in Vancouver have already found a floor they like. What they're unsure about is everything that happens after: what the quote should include, whether the old floor has to come out, how many days the house will be torn up, and how to tell a good installer from a cheap one.

This guide covers all of that for every floor type we put in. It's written for homes in [Vancouver](/flooring-in-vancouver-wa/), the rest of Clark County and the Portland side of the river. For the material-by-material detail, each section links to a deeper guide.

> **The short version:** a fair flooring installation quote is per square foot *installed*, lists tear-out, prep, underlayment, trim and disposal as separate lines, and comes after someone has measured your rooms and checked your subfloor. If a quote is missing any of those, you're not comparing like with like.

## What "flooring installation" should include

The cheapest number you see is usually the material alone. A complete installation has six parts, and each one belongs on the quote:

1. **Measure and subfloor check.** Someone measures every room, notes doorways, closets and stairs, and checks the floor for flatness, squeaks, soft spots and moisture. On a concrete slab that means a moisture test. Without this step, any price is a guess.
2. **Furniture moving.** Standard household furniture is moved out of the way and put back. Our crews do this at no extra charge.
3. **Tear-out and disposal.** Pulling up carpet and pad, tack strip, old vinyl or laminate, and hauling it away. This is often a separate line, so look for it.
4. **Subfloor prep.** Screwing down squeaks, replacing soft or water-damaged plywood, grinding high spots and filling low spots with patch or self-leveler. Prep is what decides whether a floor lasts. It is also the item most often left off a low bid.
5. **The installation itself**, with the right underlayment, pad or moisture barrier for that floor.
6. **Finish work.** [Baseboard or quarter round](/baseboards-trim/), transitions to other floors, stair nosing, door undercuts if the new floor is taller, and cleanup.

If you get two quotes and one is a lot lower, check these six lines first. The difference is almost always prep, tear-out or trim that one company priced and the other left out. ## How each type of floor is installed

The method changes with the material, and so does what has to be true about the floor underneath.

| Floor | How it's installed | What it needs underneath | Typical time, one average room |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carpet | Stretched over pad onto tack strip, seams hot-melt taped | Clean, sound subfloor. Old carpet and pad always come out | Half a day to one day |
| Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) | Floating click-lock, or glue-down in commercial and some kitchens | Very flat (about 3/16" over 10 ft for most brands) | One day |
| Laminate | Floating click-lock over foam underlayment | Flat, plus a vapor barrier on concrete | One day |
| Hardwood | Nail-down over plywood; glue-down or floating over concrete | Dry, flat, moisture-tested subfloor | One to two days, after acclimation |
| Tile | Set in thin-set mortar over cement board or an uncoupling membrane | Stiff, flat subfloor. Large tile needs it flatter still | Two to three days including cure and grout |

### Carpet

Carpet installation is mostly about the pad, the stretch and the seams. A power stretcher (not just a knee kicker) is what keeps wall-to-wall carpet from rippling in a year or two. Seams should run away from the main light source and out of walkways. Stairs are priced per step. More on that in [carpet installation in Vancouver, WA](/blog/carpet-installation-vancouver-wa/), and on the products at [carpet flooring](/carpet-flooring/).

### Luxury vinyl plank

[Luxury vinyl plank](/luxury-vinyl-plank/) is the most-installed floor in Clark County right now. It's waterproof, it can float over many existing floors, and a crew can do a whole main floor in two or three days. The catch is flatness. Rigid-core planks bridge small dips but not humps, and an unflat floor shows up as clicking joints and planks that separate. Floating vs. glue-down, expansion gaps and transitions are covered in [LVP installation in Vancouver, WA](/blog/luxury-vinyl-plank-installation-vancouver-wa/).

### Laminate

[Laminate](/laminate-flooring/) installs a lot like LVP but is less forgiving of water and of moisture coming up through concrete. Over a slab or above a crawlspace, the vapor barrier is not optional. Laminate needs its expansion gap at every wall and doorway, which is why it's trimmed with quarter round or undercut baseboard. Details are in [laminate flooring installation](/blog/laminate-flooring-installation-vancouver-wa/).

### Hardwood

Solid [hardwood](/hardwood-flooring/) is nailed to a plywood or OSB subfloor. Over concrete it's usually engineered hardwood, glued down or floated. In our climate the step people skip is moisture: the boards need to acclimate and the subfloor needs a moisture reading before a single nail goes in. Homes over a damp crawlspace are where hardwood goes wrong. Nail-down vs. glue-down, acclimation and timelines are in [hardwood floor installation in Vancouver, WA](/blog/hardwood-floor-installation-vancouver-wa/).

### Tile

[Tile](/tile-flooring/) is the slowest install because it's a wet trade. Mortar has to cure before grouting, and grout has to cure before the floor takes real traffic. Large-format tile (anything with a side 15 inches or longer) needs a flatter floor than standard tile. Bathrooms need a waterproofing plan. See [tile installation in Vancouver, WA](/blog/tile-installation-vancouver-wa/).

## What Vancouver and Clark County homes need underneath

The local housing stock decides a lot of the prep work. A few patterns we see again and again:

- **Crawlspace homes.** Many older houses in central Vancouver, Hazel Dell and [Salmon Creek](/flooring-in-salmon-creek-wa/) sit over crawlspaces. If the ground isn't covered with a vapor barrier, moisture works up into the subfloor. That matters most for hardwood and laminate. Check the crawlspace before choosing a floor.
- **Slab-on-grade and daylight basements.** Newer subdivisions in [Battle Ground](/flooring-in-battle-ground-wa/), [Ridgefield](/flooring-in-ridgefield-wa/) and [Camas](/flooring-in-camas-wa/) often have slab floors on the ground level. Concrete needs a moisture test and, for most floors, a vapor barrier. Our [basement flooring guide](/blog/basement-flooring-vancouver-wa-portland/) covers below-grade floors.
- **1970s to 1990s homes.** Subfloors in these homes are often particleboard under the carpet, which swells if it ever got wet and doesn't hold nails well. Sometimes it has to be replaced or covered with new plywood before hardwood or tile.
- **Old sheet vinyl.** Sheet vinyl and its adhesive in homes built before the 1980s may contain asbestos. Often the safer and cheaper path is to leave a well-bonded layer in place and float the new floor over it. When it does have to come out, it should be tested first.
- **Carpet over hardwood.** In older east Vancouver and Portland homes it's common to pull up carpet and find oak underneath. If it's in decent shape, [refinishing](/hardwood-refinishing/) may beat installing anything new. See [refinish or replace](/blog/refinish-or-replace-hardwood-floors-vancouver-wa/).

## How long flooring installation takes

For planning purposes:

- **One room, floating floor or carpet:** one day.
- **Whole main floor of LVP or laminate (800 to 1,200 sq ft):** two to three days.
- **Hardwood:** add acclimation time before install day, plus one to two days per average room on site.
- **Tile:** two to three days for a bathroom or entry. Grout cures before heavy use.
- **Lead time:** stocked products can often be installed within a couple of weeks of the estimate. Special-order lines add shipping time.

You can usually stay in the house. Plan to be out of the rooms being worked on, keep pets away from open doors and saws, and expect some dust and noise. ## What flooring installation costs in Vancouver, WA

These are installed prices (material plus standard labor) per square foot, the same ranges as our [full flooring cost guide](/blog/flooring-cost-vancouver-wa/):

- **Carpet:** about $3 to $7
- **Laminate:** about $4 to $9
- **Luxury vinyl plank:** about $6 to $13
- **Hardwood:** about $8 to $18
- **Tile:** about $8 to $20

What pushes a project toward the top of a range: tear-out of glued-down floors, leveling, replacing subfloor, stairs, lots of small rooms and closets, diagonal or herringbone layouts, and moving heavy furniture like pianos. What keeps it low: an open layout, a flat subfloor, a floating floor that can go over what's there, and doing the whole area at once so there are fewer transitions.

If the total is right but the timing isn't, look at [flooring financing](/financing/) before cutting the project down. A floor that stops at a doorway because the budget ran out costs more to finish later. Our [budget guide](/blog/flooring-on-a-budget-vancouver-wa/) covers where it's safe to save.

## Finding flooring installers near you: what to check

When you search for installers near you, you'll see four kinds of results: local flooring stores with their own crews, big-box stores that subcontract installation, shop-at-home franchises, and lead sites that sell your request to whoever pays. Any of them can do a good job. The questions that sort them out are the same:

- **Who actually installs the floor?** Ask whether the crew works regularly with the company or is a subcontractor found for this one job.
- **Is the company registered with Washington L&I?** Look it up yourself on the L&I contractor lookup. It takes a minute.
- **Did they measure in person and check the subfloor?** A quote from a phone call or a photo is a guess.
- **Is the quote itemized?** Tear-out, prep, underlayment, trim, stairs and disposal should each be visible.
- **What does the installation warranty cover, and who do you call?** A manufacturer warranty covers the product. The installer's warranty covers the work. You want both.
- **Is there a showroom you can walk into later?** If something needs attention a year from now, you want a place, not just a phone number.
