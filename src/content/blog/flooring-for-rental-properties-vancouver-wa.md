Flooring in a rental is a different decision from flooring in your own home. You are not buying the floor you love. You are buying the floor that costs the least per year of service, survives tenants you have not met yet, and can be turned over in a day between leases.

Landlords and property managers in Vancouver ask us the same handful of questions, so here are the answers we give them.

## The number that matters: cost per year, not cost per foot

A cheap floor that needs replacing at every second turnover is expensive. A mid-priced floor that lasts fifteen years through six tenants is cheap. Divide the installed cost by realistic years of service and the ranking changes.

Roughly, for a typical Vancouver rental:

- **Builder-grade carpet:** replaced every 5 to 7 years, often sooner with pets.
- **Thin LVP (6 to 8 mil wear layer):** looks tired in 3 to 5 years.
- **Quality LVP (20 mil wear layer):** 15 to 20 years with plank-level repairs along the way.
- **Porcelain tile:** the life of the building, with grout maintenance.

That is why our default recommendation for rentals is a 20 mil LVP, not the cheapest LVP in the building. Our [vinyl buyer's guide](/blog/vinyl-flooring-vancouver-wa/) explains what the wear layer number means.

## Room by room

**Living areas, kitchen, hallways, entry: luxury vinyl plank.** Waterproof, so the dishwasher leak or the overwatered plant is a wipe-up rather than a claim. Scratch-resistant enough for furniture being dragged in and out. Quiet underfoot, which matters in multi-family. And repairable one plank at a time. Read [waterproof flooring in Vancouver](/blog/waterproof-flooring-vancouver-wa/) for what the waterproof label actually covers.

**Bedrooms: carpet or LVP.** Carpet is still the lowest installed cost per room, tenants like it in bedrooms, and a bedroom carpet lives an easier life than a living-room one. Choose a solution-dyed polyester or nylon with stain resistance and, crucially, a decent pad. If you would rather never deal with carpet again, run the LVP through.

**Bathrooms and laundry: LVP or tile.** Both are waterproof. Tile lasts longer and handles standing water better; LVP is cheaper and faster to install. On a tight turnover, LVP. On a long-hold property, tile. See our [bathroom flooring guide](/blog/bathroom-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

**Stairs: carpet.** Carpeted stairs are safer, quieter, and far cheaper to install than hard-surface stairs with nosings. A remnant is often enough for a single run.

## Why not hardwood

Hardwood in a rental is a bet that every tenant will treat it like their own. Some will. The one who does not will drag a fridge across it, let a leak sit under the sink for a month, or keep a large dog on it for two years.

If the property already has hardwood, keep it and [refinish it](/hardwood-refinishing/) between tenants when it needs it; a refinish is far cheaper than any replacement and buyers and premium renters value original wood. If the property does not have hardwood, installing it for a rental rarely pays back. Our [refinish-or-replace guide](/blog/refinish-or-replace-hardwood-floors-vancouver-wa/) helps you judge what you have.

## Laminate: fine where it is dry

Laminate has one genuine advantage in a rental: surface hardness. It resists scratches better than most LVP. It also has one genuine disadvantage: a wood-fiber core that swells if water sits in the seams.

In a rental you do not control how quickly spills get wiped up. That pushes laminate to bedrooms and dry living rooms only, and pushes LVP everywhere near water. Our [LVP vs. laminate comparison](/blog/lvp-vs-laminate-rainy-climate/) goes into the detail.

## The pet question

Assume pets. Even a no-pets lease meets a tenant with a pet eventually.

That means:

- LVP wear layer of 20 mil, not 12
- Carpet with a stain-resistant fiber and a moisture-barrier pad, so accidents do not soak into the subfloor
- No hardwood

Our guide to [pet-friendly flooring](/blog/pet-friendly-scratch-resistant-flooring-vancouver-wa/) explains what claws and accidents each do to a floor.

## Standardize across your units

If you own or manage more than one property, pick one LVP line and one carpet line and use them everywhere.

The benefits compound:

- Spare cartons fit any unit, so a damaged plank is a twenty-minute repair instead of a search for a discontinued color.
- Reorders are one phone call.
- You know exactly how the product wears, so budgeting gets accurate.
- Turnover crews work faster on a product they have installed before.

Choose a **mid-tone color** with some variation in the grain. Very light floors show dirt; very dark floors show dust and scratches. A medium warm brown or a mid-gray-brown hides the most.

And choose a **line the manufacturer has carried for years**, not this season's new color. Longevity of the product line is what makes the standardization work.

## Planning the turnover install

The floor is rarely the slow part. Material lead time is.

- **Order before the tenant leaves.** If you are using a stocked line, we can have it ready for the day after move-out.
- **Book the crew for the first day the unit is empty.** A single-family rental in in-stock LVP or carpet is typically a one-to-two-day install once the unit is clear.
- **Do the whole unit at once**, not room by room. The setup cost is the same, and matching later is a headache.
- **Schedule paint and flooring in the right order.** Paint first, then floors, then final touch-up on the trim.

## What we would put in a typical Vancouver rental

For a three-bedroom single-family rental in Clark County, this is the spec we would give a property manager:

- 20 mil rigid-core LVP in a mid-tone wood look through the living area, kitchen, hall, entry, and both bathrooms
- Stain-resistant carpet with a moisture-barrier pad in the three bedrooms
- Carpet on the stairs, from the same roll if possible
- Two spare cartons of the LVP left in the garage or a closet

Installed in two days between tenants, with a realistic fifteen-year horizon on the hard surface.
