Sheet vinyl has an image problem. Most people picture the yellowed, peeling floor in a grandparent's kitchen. Today's sheet vinyl is a different product, and for some rooms it is still the best floor you can buy: one continuous waterproof sheet, soft and warm underfoot, and very affordable.

It is not the right floor for every room. This guide explains where sheet vinyl beats [luxury vinyl plank](/luxury-vinyl-plank/), where it does not, what the backing and installation choices mean, what to do about old vinyl already on the floor, and what drives the price.

For the broader vinyl picture, including LVT and rigid core, see our [vinyl flooring buyer's guide](/blog/vinyl-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

## What sheet vinyl is

Sheet vinyl comes off a roll, usually 12 feet wide, with some lines in 6- and 13-foot widths. A typical bathroom, laundry room or mudroom can be covered in a single piece. Larger rooms get one carefully placed, sealed seam.

It is built in layers: a backing, a core, a printed design layer, and a clear wear layer on top. Many products include a cushioned layer that makes it softer and warmer underfoot than tile or rigid LVP. Modern prints convincingly copy stone, tile, concrete and wood.

## Sheet vinyl vs. LVP

| | Sheet vinyl | Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seams | None or very few | A seam at every plank edge |
| Water | Most water-tight resilient floor | Waterproof planks; water can reach the subfloor through seams |
| Comfort | Soft, often cushioned | Firmer, especially rigid core |
| Look | Good stone and tile looks; wood looks less convincing | Very realistic wood, with texture |
| Subfloor tolerance | Low; shows every bump | Higher; rigid core bridges small flaws |
| Repairs | Patch or replace the sheet | Replace individual planks |
| Lifespan | 10–20 years | 20–30 years with a thick wear layer |
| Cost | Usually lower | Higher, roughly $6–$13/sq ft installed |

The pattern: sheet vinyl wins where water and budget matter most. LVP wins where looks, durability and long-term value matter most.

## Where sheet vinyl beats LVP

**Laundry rooms.** Washer leaks and overflows are the most common water damage we see. A single sheet with the edges sealed keeps water on top of the floor instead of letting it into the subfloor through seams.

**Kids' and guest bathrooms.** Bathwater on the floor every night is a seam problem for any plank product. Sheet vinyl has no seams to get through, and it is warmer underfoot than tile. Our [bathroom flooring guide](/blog/bathroom-flooring-vancouver-wa/) compares it with tile and LVP.

**Mudrooms and back entries.** Wet boots, dog paws and gravel, in a region where it rains for half the year. Sheet vinyl is easy to mop, and a scuffed mudroom floor is inexpensive to replace.

**Rental units.** For landlords, sheet vinyl is the cheapest waterproof floor to install and replace between tenants. Our guide to [flooring for rental properties](/blog/flooring-for-rental-properties-vancouver-wa/) covers when it is worth stepping up to LVP.

**Utility rooms, small kitchens and budget projects.** When the goal is a clean, waterproof floor for the least money, sheet vinyl is usually it.

## Where sheet vinyl is the wrong choice

- **Large open-plan main floors.** Wide rooms need seams, and a convincing wood look is easier to get with planks. LVP or [laminate](/laminate-flooring/) suits these spaces better.
- **Uneven subfloors that cannot be fixed.** Sheet vinyl is thin and follows whatever is under it.
- **Rooms with heavy, rolling loads.** Office chairs and appliances can dent cushioned vinyl. Use chair mats and appliance sliders.
- **Where you want the floor to add resale value.** Buyers respond to wood-look plank and tile in main living areas.

## Felt-backed vs. fiberglass-backed

This choice decides how the floor is installed.

**Felt-backed sheet vinyl** is glued down over the entire floor. Once it is bonded, it is very stable and handles rolling loads well. It needs a smooth, clean, well-prepared subfloor because the glue bond is what holds it.

**Fiberglass-backed sheet vinyl** is softer, more flexible and more dimensionally stable. It resists curling and shrinking, and depending on the product and room size it can be glued at the edges only or loose-laid. It is more forgiving of an imperfect subfloor and easier to replace later.

We pick the backing and installation method for the room and subfloor, following the manufacturer's instructions, because that is what the warranty depends on.

## Preparation decides how it looks

Because sheet vinyl is thin, it shows almost everything underneath: nail heads, seams in the old floor, dips, even a grain of grit trapped during install. That is called telegraphing, and it is the most common reason sheet vinyl looks cheap. Done properly, the prep is:

1. **Remove or assess the old floor.** If the existing floor is sound and smooth, the new vinyl can often go over it.
2. **Flatten and smooth.** Patch low spots, fill seams and set or remove fasteners. Over a rough or damaged subfloor, a thin plywood underlayment gives a clean base.
3. **Check moisture on concrete.** Basements and slab-on-grade floors are tested before anything is glued down.
4. **Template and cut.** The sheet is cut to fit the room in one piece, with seams placed out of traffic and out of sight.
5. **Seal the edges.** In bathrooms and laundry rooms, edges at the tub, toilet and walls are sealed so water stays on top.

## What to do about old sheet vinyl

If your home was built before the 1980s, the sheet vinyl and the black adhesive under it may contain asbestos. That is not a reason to panic. Intact flooring is generally not a hazard. It is a reason not to scrape, sand or tear it out until it has been tested.

Your options:

- **Test it.** An accredited lab can tell you within days whether asbestos is present.
- **Cover it.** If the old floor is well bonded, installing new flooring or an underlayment over it is often the simplest and safest route.
- **Have it removed professionally** if it has to come out and the test is positive.

We will raise this at your estimate if we see older vinyl, and we will not start removing it before it has been tested.

## What drives the cost of sheet vinyl

Sheet vinyl is usually the least expensive hard-surface floor we install, and it typically comes in below LVP on the same room. The total depends on:

- **The product.** Thicker wear layers, cushioned constructions and better prints cost more and last longer.
- **The room size and shape.** Material is cut off a 12-foot roll, so a room's dimensions decide the waste and whether a seam is needed.
- **Subfloor prep.** Underlayment, patching and fastener work are often the biggest variable on an older floor.
- **Removal of the old floor,** and testing if the old vinyl is from before the 1980s.
- **Toilet removal and reset, appliance moving and trim** in bathrooms and laundry rooms.

On small rooms the prep and labor are a bigger share of the total than the vinyl itself, so get an installed quote rather than a per-square-foot material price. Our [flooring cost guide](/blog/flooring-cost-vancouver-wa/) has ranges for the other materials if you are comparing.

## Living with sheet vinyl

- **Avoid rubber- and latex-backed mats.** They can permanently stain vinyl yellow. Use mats labeled safe for vinyl floors.
- **Slide appliances on boards or sliders,** never directly across the floor.
- **Use felt pads on furniture legs** and a mat under rolling chairs.
- **Clean with a damp mop and a pH-neutral cleaner.** Skip wax, polish and steam mops unless the manufacturer allows them.
