Clark County isn't one hardwood market. A 1950s ranch in Hazel Dell, a mill-era bungalow a few blocks from downtown Camas, a new build on Prune Hill and a hillside home in Washougal facing straight into the Columbia River Gorge all need different things from a wood floor.

The wood itself is the same. What changes is the subfloor, the sunlight, how dry the house gets in winter, and whether there's already a good floor under the carpet.

This guide goes city by city, then covers the decisions every Clark County hardwood floor shares: solid or engineered, humidity, and how to keep it clean through eight months of rain.

## Camas: original floors downtown, wide planks on the hill

Camas grew up around its paper mill, which opened in 1883, and the older homes around downtown still show it. Many have original oak or fir floors, often under carpet or old vinyl. If there is enough wood above the tongue, [refinishing](/hardwood-refinishing-flooring-in-camas-wa/) usually beats replacing on both cost and character.

Up on Prune Hill, around Lacamas Lake and in Grass Valley, the homes are much newer. Camas roughly doubled in population between 2000 and 2020, and most of that growth arrived as larger homes with open floor plans. Those homes raise different questions:

- **Wide planks.** Seven-inch and wider boards suit big open rooms, but wide solid boards move more with humidity. Wide plank is where engineered hardwood earns its place. Our [wide plank and herringbone guide](/blog/wide-plank-herringbone-flooring-vancouver-wa/) explains why.
- **Big windows and sun.** Floor-to-ceiling glass facing the lake or the Gorge will change the color of any wood floor over time. Cherry and walnut darken; many oak finishes amber. Window film or shades, moving rugs now and then, and a finish with UV inhibitors slow it down.
- **One floor through everything.** Open plans look best with the same wood running from entry to kitchen to family room. That favors engineered wood in the kitchen, where it handles spills and humidity better than solid.

## Washougal: plan for the east wind

Washougal sits at the western mouth of the Columbia River Gorge, and in winter that means east wind. When cold, dry air pours out of the Gorge, it pulls humidity out of every house it reaches, on top of the drying effect of running the heat.

Hardwood responds to that. As indoor relative humidity drops, boards shrink and gaps open between them. It is the most common hardwood question we get from Washougal and east Camas homeowners each February.

What helps:

- **Engineered over solid** in rooms with the most exposure, including river-facing lower levels and rooms with a lot of exterior wall
- **A humidifier,** whole-house or room units, to keep indoor humidity roughly between 30 and 50 percent, the range most flooring manufacturers specify
- **Narrower boards** if you want solid wood, since they show smaller gaps than wide ones
- **Proper acclimation** before installation, so the wood starts at the moisture level your house actually runs at

Small gaps that open in winter and close by late spring are normal wood behavior, not a defect. Large gaps, cupping or crowning point to a moisture problem that needs to be found. Older homes near downtown Washougal often have floors worth [refinishing](/hardwood-refinishing-flooring-in-washougal-wa/). ## Battle Ground and Ridgefield: replacing builder-grade floors

Battle Ground and Ridgefield have been two of the fastest-growing cities in Washington, and a large share of their homes were built in the last 25 years. In those houses, the hardwood conversation is usually about replacing builder-grade carpet or vinyl with real wood.

- **Check the foundation.** Homes on a slab need engineered hardwood, glued down or floated, after a moisture test on the concrete. Homes over a crawlspace with a plywood or OSB subfloor can take solid or engineered.
- **Match the stairs.** Carpeted stairs left between new hardwood floors are the most common thing homeowners regret. Our [hardwood stairs guide](/blog/hardwood-stairs-vancouver-wa/) covers treads and nosing.
- **Think about dogs.** Young families and big dogs are common in these neighborhoods. Harder species like hickory and white oak, a matte finish and a wire-brushed texture all hide scratches. See our [pet-friendly flooring guide](/blog/pet-friendly-scratch-resistant-flooring-vancouver-wa/).

## Hazel Dell and Salmon Creek: solid oak worth saving

Hazel Dell was one of the first parts of Clark County to suburbanize after World War II, and its mid-century homes are full of solid oak strip flooring. A lot of it is hiding under wall-to-wall carpet.

Before pricing new floors, pull up a corner of carpet in a closet. Three-quarter-inch solid oak has a lot of life left in it. It can usually be sanded several times, and a sand and refinish, with a new stain color if you want one, costs much less than replacing it. Our guide to [refinishing or replacing hardwood](/blog/refinish-or-replace-hardwood-floors-vancouver-wa/) has a thickness check you can do yourself.

When rooms need new wood to match, such as an addition, a kitchen where vinyl was put down, or a hallway that was patched, solid oak can be woven into the existing floor and the whole area sanded and finished together so the new boards disappear. This is where solid hardwood makes the most sense. See [hardwood flooring in Hazel Dell](/hardwood-flooring-in-hazel-dell-wa/) and [in Salmon Creek](/hardwood-flooring-in-salmon-creek-wa/).

## Keeping hardwood clean through the wet season

Most of the wear we see on hardwood in Camas, Battle Ground and the rest of the county comes from grit tracked in from October to May. Wet sand and gravel on shoes and paws act like sandpaper on the finish.

- **Mats at every door,** outside and in, plus a boot tray so wet shoes don't sit on the wood
- **Dry clean often.** A microfiber dust mop or a vacuum with the beater bar turned off, several times a week in the rainy season
- **Damp, never wet.** A lightly damp microfiber mop with a pH-neutral cleaner made for finished wood floors
- **Skip** steam mops, vinegar, oil soaps and wax products. They dull or damage polyurethane finishes and can make a future recoat harder.
- **Felt pads** on chair and table legs, replaced when they pick up grit
- **Recoat before it wears through.** When the finish looks dull in the traffic lanes but the wood isn't scratched through, a screen and recoat restores it without a full sand.

## What drives the cost

The wood is only part of the price. In Clark County homes, these move the number most:

- **Solid vs. engineered,** species, grade and plank width
- **Subfloor work,** including leveling, moisture testing on slabs, and replacing damaged sections
- **Tear-out** of carpet, vinyl or old floating floors
- **Stairs,** which are priced per step
- **Weaving** new boards into existing floors, and refinishing them together
